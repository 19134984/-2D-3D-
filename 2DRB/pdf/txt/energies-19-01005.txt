An Improved Lattice Boltzmann Method for Simulating
High-Conductivity-Ratio Conjugate Heat Transfer
Zuoming Yang 1,2 , Renbao Zhao 1, * , Juanyong Wang 3 , Fang Xiong 3 and Lei Wang 3

                                         1   College of Petroleum Engineering, China University of Petroleum, Beijing 100100, China

                                         *   Correspondence: zhaorb@cup.edu.cn or renbaozhao@126.com


                                         Abstract
                                         This paper presents an improved lattice Boltzmann method for simulating conjugate
                                         heat transfer. In conventional lattice Boltzmann methods, the coupling between thermal
                                         conductivity and relaxation time limits numerical stability for high-conductivity solids.
                                         To address this challenge, the proposed model introduces a correction source term to
                                         decouple the physical thermal conductivity from the relaxation parameter. The method
                                         is validated through representative test cases including heat conduction in a two-layered
                                         annulus, conjugate heat transfer in a layered channel, and natural convection in a square
                                         enclosure with discrete solid blocks. Numerical results demonstrate that the algorithm
                                         possesses second-order spatial accuracy. Moreover, the results show good agreement with
                                         the literature benchmarks at low thermal conductivity ratios and confirm the capability
                                         of the method to simulate conjugate heat transfer at high thermal conductivity ratios up
                                         to 1000.

                                         Keywords: high-conductivity-ratio; conjugate heat transfer; lattice Boltzmann method




                                         1. Introduction
                                               In modern industrial applications, such as high-performance integrated circuit cool-
                                         ing [1] and thermal management for new energy vehicle batteries [2,3], the cross-medium
                                         transfer and diffusion of heat are central to system reliability. The essence of these problems
                                         lies in the conjugate heat transfer (CHT) between fluid and solid domains [4]. To accu-
                                         rately predict energy exchange at these complex interfaces, various numerical techniques
                                         have been developed. Traditional macroscopic algorithms, such as the Finite Difference
                                         Method [5] and Finite Volume Method [6,7], are well established for CHT in conventional
                                         geometries. However, these methods often encounter challenges like complex grid gen-

                          complex heat exchangers [17,18]. Specifically, Gao et al. [11] proposed a modified LBM
                          for porous media that ensures temperature and heat flux continuity by incorporating vol-
                          umetric heat capacity into the equilibrium distribution function, bypassing the need for
                          additional interface corrections. Lu et al. [19] developed a unified thermal LBM based on a
                          virtual heat capacity correction method, effectively addressing CHT across both flat and
                          curved boundaries within an optimal two-relaxation-time framework. Hosseini et al. [20]
                          developed an advection-diffusion model specifically for heterogeneous media, effectively
                          handling the jump conditions at interfaces. Furthermore, Korba et al. [21] conducted a
                          comprehensive study on the accuracy of various interface schemes, establishing rigorous
                          benchmarks for mass and heat transfer across conjugate boundaries. These efforts have
                          significantly expanded the application of LBM in the field of fluid–solid coupled thermal
                          transport and established it as an efficient numerical tool for CHT.
                                However, despite significant progress in LBM for CHT, current algorithmic research
                          has paid limited attention to numerical robustness in the presence of extreme thermal
                          property contrasts [11]. A quantitative review of recent studies reveals that most pure
                          LBM models are validated only for moderate thermal conductivity ratios (κd ). For exam-
                          ple, the enthalpy-based cascaded LBM proposed by Liu et al. [22] successfully ensures
                          automatic interface continuity, yet their validation benchmarks were typically limited to
                          conductivity ratios of κd ≤ 100. Similarly, in a very recent study by Nejadseifi et al. [23],
                          the maximum conductivity ratio investigated for a pure LBM solver was limited to 400. In
                          industrial scenarios involving high-performance heat spreaders (e.g., copper–air systems),
                          the conductivity ratio often exceeds 1000. Under such extreme contrasts, the intrinsic cou-
                          pling between the physical thermal conductivity and the relaxation time in standard LBM
                          frameworks leads to prohibitively large relaxation parameters [10]. The intrinsic coupling
                          between physical thermal conductivity and the relaxation parameter often triggers severe
                          numerical instabilities or even computational divergence as the relaxation time approaches
                          its theoretical stability limit [24,25]. To circumvent this, some researchers have resorted
                          to hybrid approaches. For example, Benhamou et al. [26] combined LBM with the Finite
                          Difference Method to maintain stability. While effective, such hybrid methods compromise
                          the inherent parallelism and mesoscopic unity of the lattice Boltzmann framework. There-
                          fore, developing a pure LBM algorithm capable of decoupling the thermal conductivity
                          from the relaxation time constraint while maintaining robustness is of substantial academic
                          and engineering value for broadening the industrial utility of LBM.
                                To overcome the aforementioned numerical stability bottlenecks caused by the cou-
                          pling between high thermal conductivity and relaxation time, this work proposes an
                          improved lattice Boltzmann model for single-phase fluid–solid conjugate heat transfer. The
                          proposed model introduces a thermophysical property correction operator to decouple the
                          thermal conductivity from the collision relaxation time. This allows for robust simulations
                          of conjugate heat transfer with conductivity ratios as high as 1000 without compromising
                          stability. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 details the numerical
                          framework comprising the standard fluid solver and the improved decoupled thermal
                          model; Section 3 validates the accuracy and robustness of the algorithm under extreme
                          thermal property contrasts; and Section 4 summarizes the main conclusions.

                          2. Numerical Method
                               In this study, a DDF framework is utilized [10]. The density and velocity fields
                          are solved using the standard lattice Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (LBGK) model, while the
                          temperature field is solved using an improved thermal lattice Boltzmann model adapted
                          for solid–liquid conjugate heat transfer.

                          3.3. Natural Convection in a Square Cavity Filled with 16 Solid Blocks
                               To further evaluate the numerical performance of the proposed LBM algorithm in
                          handling complex geometries and a wide range of thermal conductivity ratios (κd ), this
                          section investigates the problem of conjugate natural convection within a square enclosure
                          containing 16 discrete solid cylinders. Figure 6 illustrates the geometric configuration,

                          which consists of a square cavity populated with 16 uniformly arranged solid cylinders of
                          identical radius. The left vertical wall is maintained at a high temperature Th = 1.0, while
                          the right vertical wall is kept at a low temperature Tc = 0.0. The top and bottom walls are
                          adiabatic. In our simulations, the non-equilibrium extrapolation scheme are applied in
                          all boundaries [30]. The Prandtl number of the fluid is set to Pr = 0.7, and the Rayleigh
                          number is fixed at Ra = 105 . The thermal conductivities of the solid and fluid are denoted
                          by κ1 and κ2 , respectively, with the conductivity ratio defined as κd = κ1 /κ2 . Driven by
                          buoyancy forces, the fluid undergoes natural convection, resulting in intense conjugate
                          heat exchange between the fluid and the discrete solid domains.

                          4. Conclusions
                               This research introduces a refined LB framework tailored to address CHT problems
                          characterized by large thermal conductivity ratios. Unlike conventional LB model ap-
                          proaches that often exhibit numerical instability or significant errors when the thermal
                          properties of the solid and fluid phases differ drastically, the present model is designed
                          to maintain robustness in high-contrast regimes. The effectiveness of this approach is
                          verified by our simulations, which confirm its capability to precisely resolve interfacial
                          thermal profiles and fluxes amidst extreme conductivity disparities, thereby surpassing the
                          constraints identified in the earlier literature.
                               The validity and accuracy of the proposed model were systematically verified through
                          simulations of three distinct scenarios: conjugate heat transfer problems with available
                          analytical solutions, the evaluation of heat transfer characteristics and thermal performance
                          under different geometric configurations, and natural convection within a square enclosure


                                                                                            https://doi.org/10.3390/en19041005
Energies 2026, 19, 1005                                                                                                                13 of 14



                                   containing 16 discrete solid blocks. Quantitative scrutiny reveals that our computational
                                   results exhibit excellent agreement with both the established literature and theoretical
                                   benchmarks, yielding spatial convergence of the second order. These test cases confirm that
                                   the model is capable of handling complex geometries and significant property variations
                                   with high fidelity. Importantly, the scope of this research is currently limited to heat
                                   transport within monophasic fluids and stationary solid domains. Looking ahead, we
                                   intend to broaden this numerical approach to address sophisticated engineering challenges,
                                   including thermal regulation within stochastic porous structures.
