A hybrid lattice Boltzmann/immersed boundary method/finite-difference
model for thermal fluid-solid interactions

Keywords:                                                 Particulate flows are frequently observed in both industrial and natural environments, and their simulation
Lattice Boltzmann method                                  becomes particularly challenging when heat transfer is involved. This study introduces a hybrid numerical
Hybrid solver                                             approach, combining the lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM), Immersed Boundary Method (IBM), and Finite Dif­
Heat transfer
                                                          ference (FD) energy solver to alleviate the limitations observed in double distributed lattice Boltzmann methods
Central moments
                                                          and classical Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). The model incorporates an enhanced Hermite polynomial-
                                                          based central moments collision operator. This enhancement boosts stability and overcomes the Galilean-
                                                          invariant issues observed in classical LBM. For heat transfer, a Finite-Difference technique is employed for
                                                          resolving the energy equation, discretizing the advection term through a Weighted Essentially Non-Oscillatory
                                                          (WENO) approach. The Boussinesq approximation handles buoyancy forces, while the direct-forcing IBM man­
                                                          ages fluid-particle interaction. Validation across various test cases confirms the model’s accuracy, showcasing its
                                                          ability to account for variations in thermal properties like specific heat capacity Cp over a wide range. The model
                                                          is further utilized to investigate a reactor containing 300 catalyst particles. In summary, this hybrid methodology
                                                          is found to be a promising tool for comprehensively studying and designing particle-laden systems with thermal
                                                          effects.

2.4. Solver overall structure

   To simulate the behavior of a system involving fluid dynamics, heat
transfer, and particle motion, the following algorithm has been devel­
oped to couple all models together. Fig. 2 illustrates the algorithm for
the proposed approach, and the step-by-step details of this algorithm are
elaborated in the following.

1. Calculating the Boussinesq term: In every time step, the Boussinesq




                                                                                                  h




                                                                                                                                                               c
   term is calculated using Eq. (23). This term takes into account the
   buoyancy forces resulting from the Boussinesq approximation.
2. Solving the fluid domain: The fluid flow is solved using Eq. (1),
   which represents the corresponding governing equation. This step
   provides pressure and velocity of fluid field. The corresponding
   calculations rely on the particle and heat forces from the previous
   time step.
3. Solving the energy equation: To compute the temperature distribu­
   tion within the fluid domain, the energy Eq. (22) is calculated, taking
   into account the just updated fluid velocity, and the particle heat
   forcing acquired from the preceding time step.
4. Calculating particle forces and spreading to Eulerian nodes: Particle
   forces are computed utilizing Eqs. (39), (33), and (31), incorporating
   interactions between particles, fluid-particle interactions, and
   external forces. Subsequently, these forces are distributed to the
   Eulerian nodes using Eq. (37). These calculations rely on the updated                 Fig. 3. The geometric characteristics of the composite medium.
   velocity from Step 2.
5. Calculating particle temperature, thermal forcing, and spreading to
   Eulerian nodes: Utilizing Eqs. (34) and (45), thermal forcing terms
   are computed, incorporating the updated temperature field in the
   fluid. Subsequently, these thermal forces are propagated to the
   Eulerian nodes using Eq. (38), used to compute effective fluid-
   particles heat transfer. The uniform particle temperature, in turn,
   is updated through the application of Eq. (47).
6. Motion of particles: Particle motion is controlled by Eqs. (42), (43),
   and (44), which deliver the linear and rotational particles’ velocity in
   response to the applied forces.

    By adhering to this algorithmic flow, the three solvers successfully
communicate and exchange information at each time step, ensuring the
proper coupling between fluid dynamics, heat transfer, and particle
motion.
