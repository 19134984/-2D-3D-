Keywords:                                                   Enhancing convective heat transfer in fluid systems is crucial for improving energy efficiency in both natural
Rayleigh–Bénard convection                                  and industrial processes. One canonical example of such systems is Rayleigh–Bénard convection, which involves
Heat transfer enhancement                                   buoyancy-driven flow arising from a fluid layer heated from below and cooled from above. We numerically
Oscillatory shear
                                                            investigate two-dimensional convection with laterally oscillating sidewalls at a fixed Prandtl number (Pr = 5.3),
Thermal plume dynamics
                                                            unit aspect ratio (Γ = 1), and Rayleigh numbers (Ra) of 106 and 107 , representing moderately turbulent regimes.
                                                            The sidewalls undergo sinusoidal oscillations with amplitudes 𝜀∗ ranging from 0 to 1.7% of the free-fall velocity
                                                            scale. Despite the weak nature of this boundary forcing, it induces profound changes in the flow dynamics
                                                            by disrupting the classical large-scale circulation and promoting the formation of multiple smaller, coherent
                                                            convection cells. This structural reorganization significantly enhances thermal plume activity and transport
                                                            efficiency. Quantitatively, the Nusselt number (Nu) increases from 7.22 to 19.42 at Ra = 106 , representing
                                                            a 168.98% improvement as 𝜀∗ increases from 0 to 0.017. At Ra = 107 , Nu increases from 14.16 to 29.80,
                                                            corresponding to a 110% enhancement over the unforced case. These gains are accompanied by up to 50%
                                                            increases in the Reynolds number (Re), reflecting intensified flow velocity and turbulence. The results reveal
                                                            that the enhancement mechanism is driven by the synergistic interaction between buoyancy and oscillatory
                                                            shear, leading to boundary layer destabilization and more organized plume release. This transition from a
                                                            dissipation-dominated regime to one dominated by amplification with increasing Ra provides new insights into
                                                            energy transport in modulated convection. Our findings demonstrate that even weak lateral forcing can serve
                                                            as an effective, non-intrusive control strategy for convective heat transfer enhancement and offer promising
                                                            directions for extending this approach to three-dimensional or high-Ra systems.
