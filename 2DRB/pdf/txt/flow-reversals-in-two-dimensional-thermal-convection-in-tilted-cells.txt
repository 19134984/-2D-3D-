                                                                                      The influence of tilt on flow reversals in two-dimensional thermal convection in
                                                                                      rectangular cells with two typical aspect ratios, Γ = width/height = 1 and 2, are
                                                                                      investigated by means of direct numerical simulations. For Γ = 1, tilt tends to
                                                                                      suppress flow reversals. However, it is found that flow reversals characterized by two
                                                                                      main rolls are promoted by tilt for Γ = 2, which are even observed for some cases
                                                                                      of small Prandtl numbers (Pr) and large tilt angles (β). Different from level cases
                                                                                      where the four corner rolls all have opportunities to grow and trigger a flow reversal,
                                                                                      the reversals in an anticlockwise tilted cell with Γ = 2 are always led by the growth
                                                                                      of the bottom-right or the top-left corner roll. Tilt is favourable for the growth of
                                                                                      the bottom-right or the top-left corner roll and thus for breaking the balance between
                                                                                      the two main rolls and triggering a flow reversal. The mode decomposition analysis
                                                                                      shows that the appearance of the intermediate single-roll mode is crucial for reversals,
                                                                                      and flow reversals in a tilted cell with Γ = 2 can be viewed as a mode competition
                                                                                      process between single-roll mode and horizontally adjacent double-roll mode. They
                                                                                      can only occur in a limited range of β where the two modes have comparable
                                                                                      strength. Furthermore, the Nusselt numbers at the hot plate Nuh and at the cold plate
                                                                                      Nuc behave differently during a flow reversal for Γ = 2 due to the preference of
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2018.451 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      single corner roll growth.
                                                                                      Key words: Bénard convection, convection, convection in cavities




                                                                                      1. Introduction
                                                                                        Spontaneous reversal phenomena are common in many buoyancy-driven fluid
                                                                                      dynamical systems, including reversals of the Earth’s geomagnetic field (Glatzmaier
                                                                                      & Roberts 1995), and large-scale fluid motion in the sea and the atmosphere (van
                                                                                      Doorn et al. 2000). There also exist flow reversals of the large-scale circulation (LSC)
                                                                                      in a model problem, i.e. Rayleigh–Bénard convection (RBC) where a fluid layer is
                                                                                      heated from below and cooled from above (see Ahlers, Grossmann & Lohse 2009;
                                                                                      Xia 2013; Verma, Kumar & Pandey 2017, and references therein). The study of flow

                                                                                      356       Q. Wang, S.-N. Xia, B.-F. Wang, D.-J. Sun, Q. Zhou and Z.-H. Wan
                                                                                      reversals in a model system like RBC may enable us to gain more insights into
                                                                                      general reversal phenomena in nature (Huang et al. 2015).
                                                                                         In cylindrical cells, there are two categories of flow reversals (Brown, Nikolaenko
                                                                                      & Ahlers 2005): one is rotation-led reversals, which are caused by incessant azimuthal
                                                                                      meandering of the circulating plane; the other is cessation-led reversals, in which the
                                                                                      LSC slows to a stop and restarts in a random new orientation. Flow reversals in
                                                                                      cylindrical cells have been studied both experimentally (Brown et al. 2005; Brown
                                                                                      & Ahlers 2006; Xi, Zhou & Xia 2006; Xi & Xia 2007, 2008a,b; Xi et al. 2016)
                                                                                      and numerically (Mishra et al. 2011). Several models, either stochastic ones (Benzi
                                                                                      2005; Brown & Ahlers 2007) or deterministic ones (Araujo, Grossmann & Lohse
                                                                                      2005), have been proposed to account for the flow reversals. The model proposed
                                                                                      by Brown & Ahlers (2007) consists of two stochastic ordinary differential equations,
                                                                                      and it agrees well with many experimental results for LSC dynamics. The recent
                                                                                      experimental work of Xi et al. (2016) found that the second mode and the remaining
                                                                                      higher-order modes play important roles in the dynamical process of the reversal and
                                                                                      the Nusselt number Nu drops to its minimum during a flow reversal. It was once
                                                                                      thought that cessation-led reversals presumably would prevail in systems where the
                                                                                      rotational invariance is broken (Brown et al. 2005). However, the cessations of the
                                                                                      LSC have not been observed in a cubic cell where the rotational invariance is broken
                                                                                      (Foroozani et al. 2017). It is still ambiguous why cessations are absent in a cubic cell.
                                                                                         Without complicated three-dimensional (3D) structures, flow reversals in two-
                                                                                      dimensional (2D) (Sugiyama et al. 2010; Chandra & Verma 2011, 2013; Petschel
                                                                                      et al. 2011; Podvin & Sergent 2015; Verma, Ambhire & Pandey 2015; Castillo-
                                                                                      Castellanos, Sergent & Rossi 2016; Xia et al. 2016; Mannattil et al. 2017; Podvin
                                                                                      & Sergent 2017) or quasi-2D (Huang et al. 2015; Ni, Huang & Xia 2015; Huang &
                                                                                      Xia 2016) square cavities have attracted much attention in recent years. Sugiyama
                                                                                      et al. (2010) studied flow reversals in a square cavity with unit aspect ratio by means
                                                                                      of 2D direct numerical simulations (DNS) combined with experiments performed in
                                                                                      a quasi-2D cell, and found that the results of experiments and simulations are in very
                                                                                      good agreement. They also showed that the growth of corner rolls both in kinetic
                                                                                      energy and in size plays key roles in triggering flow reversals. They concluded that
                                                                                      flow reversals in a 2D/quasi-2D cell occur only in a limited parameter space, that
                                                                                      is to say, the reversals do not exist for too large or too small Pr as well as too
                                                                                      large or too small Ra. Chandra & Verma (2011, 2013) used a mode decomposition
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2018.451 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      method to study flow reversals, and showed that flow reversals are caused by a
                                                                                      vortex reconnection of two attracting rolls and the appearance of the (2, 2) mode
                                                                                      is critical to trigger flow reversals. Podvin & Sergent (2015, 2017) used proper
                                                                                      orthogonal decomposition to study flow reversals in a 2D square cell and proposed
                                                                                      a 3D model that is based on the interaction of the LSC, the quadrupolar flow
                                                                                      and horizontal rolls. Castillo-Castellanos et al. (2016) found that the reversal cycle is
                                                                                      composed of three successive phases: acceleration, accumulation and release including
                                                                                      a rebound event. Huang & Xia (2016) experimentally found that the frequency of flow
                                                                                      reversals increases drastically in smaller Γ cells. It should be noted that 2D/quasi-2D
                                                                                      convection promotes reversals particularly for topological reasons. In 3D cubic cells,
                                                                                      reversals are very rare (Foroozani et al. 2017). Previous works are almost restricted
                                                                                      to a unit-aspect-ratio cell where there exists only one LSC that spans the size of the
                                                                                      convection cell. It has already been realized by Sugiyama et al. (2010) that Γ has
                                                                                      a strong effect on the reversal dynamics. For a Γ = 2 cell, the flow is characterized
                                                                                      by two main rolls and flow reversals also exist (Chandra & Verma 2011; Verma
                                                                                      et al. 2015). However, much less work has so far been devoted to flow reversals in a
                                                                                      Γ = 2 cell.
                                                                                                    Flow reversals in 2D thermal convection in tilted cells              357
                                                                                         There are several differences between flow reversals in 2D/quasi-2D and 3D
                                                                                      geometries. Firstly, the mean time between successive reversals has a strong Ra
                                                                                      dependence in the former (Sugiyama et al. 2010) while it has a weak Ra dependence
                                                                                      in the latter (Brown & Ahlers 2006; Xi & Xia 2007). Secondly, flow reversals in
                                                                                      2D/quasi-2D cells occur in a limited parameter space, and there are no reversals for
                                                                                      Ra larger than 5 × 108 (2D) or 1.6 × 109 (quasi-2D) (Sugiyama et al. 2010). However,
                                                                                      flow reversals in 3D cylindrical cells can occur at larger Ra (Ra > 1010 ) (Brown et al.
                                                                                      2005; Brown & Ahlers 2006; Xi et al. 2016) and high Pr (Pr = 19.4) (Xie, Wei &
                                                                                      Xia 2013). Whether 2D reversals and cessation-led reversals correspond to the same
                                                                                      phenomenon is still an open question (Castillo-Castellanos et al. 2016).
                                                                                         Almost all previous studies are conducted in a symmetrical system. However,
                                                                                      flow reversals in the real world usually take place in an asymmetrical system. One
                                                                                      example is that the heat flux on the core–mantle boundary in the Earth is non-uniform
                                                                                      (Gubbins et al. 2011) and this has a great influence on the frequency of geomagnetic
                                                                                      reversals (Glatzmaier et al. 1999). Another is the solar convection zone that has both
                                                                                      torsional oscillations and periodic reversals (Howard & LaBonte 1980). Therefore, it
                                                                                      is important to study the influence of symmetry breaking on flow reversals. Brown
                                                                                      & Ahlers (2006, 2008) found that flow reversals in a Γ = 1 cylindrical container are
                                                                                      suppressed significantly even for very small tilt angles. Sun, Xi & Xia (2005) also
                                                                                      found that, in a cylindrical container with Γ = 0.5, a minor tilt of 2◦ will lock the
                                                                                      LSC in the azimuthal plane along the tilted direction, and reversals are suppressed;
                                                                                      similar results were also found in a rectangular cell with Γ = 0.5 (Wang et al. 2017).
                                                                                      Xia et al. (2016) studied non-Oberbeck-Boussinesq (NOB) effects on flow reversals,
                                                                                      and, interestingly, they found that, when considering NOB effects, flow reversals can
                                                                                      be observed for relatively small Pr = 0.71. Huang et al. (2015) studied the influence
                                                                                      of thermal boundary conditions on flow reversals. They claimed that the reversal of
                                                                                      the LSC can be viewed as a process for the system to restore its symmetry in a
                                                                                      statistical way.
                                                                                         It is generally accepted that tilt will suppress flow reversals in a Γ = 1 cell.
                                                                                      However, how cell tilt will influence flow reversals in larger-aspect-ratio cells where
                                                                                      multiple convection rolls may exist is still an open question. In this paper, we try
                                                                                      to fill this gap by studying the influence of cell tilt on flow reversals in a 2D
                                                                                      rectangular cell with Γ = 2 where two convection rolls may develop; the results are
                                                                                      also compared with those for Γ = 1 cases. The reason why we choose Γ = 2 is that
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2018.451 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      it has been found previously that flow reversals of a double-roll state exist in a Γ = 2
                                                                                      cell (Chandra & Verma 2011; Verma et al. 2015), while whether flow reversals
                                                                                      exist for Γ > 2 is still unknown. There are two advantages of 2D DNS: (i) The
                                                                                      numerical effort required for 2D simulations is much smaller and thus we can study
                                                                                      flow reversals in a considerable fraction of the Rayleigh number Ra/Prandtl number
                                                                                      Pr versus tilt angle β parameter space for different Γ . (ii) The flow visualization is
                                                                                      much easier, and a detailed flow reversal scenario can be obtained. In recent years,
                                                                                      2D DNS has been used widely to study turbulent convection. For example, it has
                                                                                      been used to study turbulent heat transfer (van der Poel, Stevens & Lohse 2011; van
                                                                                      der Poel et al. 2012, 2014; Huang & Zhou 2013; Bao et al. 2015), to study boundary
                                                                                      layer (BL) dynamics (Zhou et al. 2010, 2011; van der Poel et al. 2015; Zhang et al.
                                                                                      2017a), to reveal NOB effects on flow organization (Sugiyama et al. 2009), to probe
                                                                                      the effect of roughness on heat transfer (Shishkina & Wagner 2011; Toppaladoddi,
                                                                                      Succi & Wettlaufer 2017; Zhu et al. 2017; Xu et al. 2018; Zhang et al. 2018), to
                                                                                      investigate statistics of kinetic and thermal energy dissipation rates in RBC (Zhang,
                                                                                      Zhou & Sun 2017b) and, more recently, to explore the transition to the ultimate
                                                                                      regime (Zhu et al. 2018).

                                                                                         The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In § 2, we introduce the
                                                                                      governing equations and numerical methods. The main results are presented in § 3,
                                                                                      which is divided into three parts. Section 3.1 studies the influence of tilt on flow
                                                                                      reversals in Γ = 1 and Γ = 2 cells. In § 3.2, we use a mode decomposition method
                                                                                      to reveal the inherent mechanism for the reversals in a tilted Γ = 2 cell. Section 3.3
                                                                                      discusses the heat transfer properties during the reversals. Finally, a short summary
                                                                                      is presented in § 4.

                                                                                      3. Results and discussion
                                                                                                         3.1. Flow reversals in two-dimensional tilted cells
                                                                                      We start by studying the influence of tilt on flow reversals for a typical aspect ratio
                                                                                      Γ = 1. Figure 1 shows snapshots of the temperature and velocity fields during one of
                                                                                      the reversals at Ra = 5 × 107 , Pr = 2 and β = 6◦ . Although the symmetry of the system
                                                                                      is broken, it is found that the reversal process is similar to the level case (Sugiyama
                                                                                      et al. 2010). The two corner rolls grow simultaneously in kinetic energy and also in
                                                                                      size due to the plume detachment from the boundary layers, until they merge as a new
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2018.451 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      LSC with the opposite direction, and a flow reversal happens. It is well known that
                                                                                      flow reversals occur in a certain Ra–Pr parameter space for Γ = 1. For a system with
                                                                                      only one main roll, the reversal process can be nicely indicated by a global quantity,
                                                                                      i.e. the net angular momentum

                                                                                      where h·iV represents averaging over the total area. Figure 2 shows the time evolution
                                                                                      of L for the level case with β = 0◦ and the tilted case with β = 6◦ at Ra = 5 × 107 and
                                                                                      Pr = 2. A sign change of L can reveal a reversal; frequent flow reversals in a level

                                                                                         We then map out phase diagrams for Γ = 1 over β–Pr parameter space with Ra =
                                                                                      5 × 107 and over β–Ra parameter space with Pr = 2 in figure 3. We run for at least
                                                                                      5000 large-eddy turnover times tE to detect whether flow reversals exist. Time tE is
                                                                                      defined by tE := 4π/h|ωc (t)|i, where ωc denotes vorticity in the centre of the main
                                                                                      roll. For some cases (for example, Ra = 108 , Pr = 2, Γ = 1, β = 3◦ ), we even
                                                                                      calculate for more than 10 000 tE to conclude there are indeed no reversals. As shown
                                                                                      in figure 3(a), the critical angle βc displays a non-monotonic relation with Pr. For
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2018.451 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      larger Pr, the βc value is smaller; for Pr = 5, a minor tilt of 1◦ will completely
                                                                                      suppress flow reversals due to the fact that the thickness of the thermal BL is much
                                                                                      smaller than the thickness of the kinetic BL and thermal coupling between the corner
                                                                                      roll and the thermal BL is weakened, as stated previously (Sugiyama et al. 2010). For
                                                                                      small Pr, thermal diffusion becomes dominant and suppresses the growth of the corner
                                                                                      roll. Therefore, flow reversals in the tilted cell also occur more easily at intermediate
                                                                                      Pr, and the corresponding βc is also larger. In figure 3(b), βc also displays a non-
                                                                                      monotonic relation with Ra. For intermediate Ra, the corresponding βc is larger. The
                                                                                      reason lies in the non-monotonic dependence of the flow reversal frequency on Ra. It
                                                                                      can be seen in figure 3 of Sugiyama et al. (2010) that flow reversals are most frequent
                                                                                      for intermediate Ra (approximately 7 × 107 for Pr = 4.3); any increase or decrease of
                                                                                      Ra will reduce the flow reversal frequency. For the Pr = 2 considered in our work,
                                                                                      the reversals are most frequent for Ra ∼ 4 × 107 , thus the corresponding βc is larger,
                                                                                      and any increase or decrease of Ra will reduce βc . The reversals occur less and less
                                                                                      frequently with increasing Ra and reversals disappear when Ra ⩾ 2 × 108 for Pr = 2
                                                                                      (Sugiyama et al. 2010). Thus the main roll here is more sensitive to tilt at larger Ra,
                                                                                      and a minor tilt of the cell will suppress flow reversals at relatively high Ra.

                                                                                      4. Summary
                                                                                         In this work, we investigate the effect of cell tilt on flow reversals in Γ = 1 and
                                                                                      2 cells by performing 2D DNS. It is found that flow reversals are suppressed by
                                                                                      tilt for Γ = 1. However, flow reversals characterized by two main rolls are greatly
                                                                                      promoted by tilt for Γ = 2. For Γ = 1, flow reversals in tilted cells are led by the
                                                                                                       Flow reversals in 2D thermal convection in tilted cells                          369
                                                                                      simultaneous growth of two corner rolls, which is qualitatively the same as those in
                                                                                      level cases. For Γ = 2, due to the introduction of a horizontal buoyancy force by tilt,
                                                                                      flow reversals in a tilted cell are always led by the growth of the bottom-right or
                                                                                      the top-left corner roll, which is different from those in a level case, where the four
                                                                                      corner rolls all have opportunities to grow and trigger a reversal. Tilt is favourable for
                                                                                      the thermal plumes to feed the bottom-right or the top-left corner roll, thus breaking
                                                                                      the balance between the two main rolls and leading to a reversal. As a result, the
                                                                                      reversals are promoted by tilt and are much more frequent than those in level cells.
                                                                                      Moreover, flow reversals in tilted cells with Γ = 2 tend to occur for small Pr. Tilt
                                                                                      broadens the Ra–Pr parameter space for flow reversals to happen in a Γ = 2 cell. Our
                                                                                      study expands the flow reversals in a canonical system to small Pr, which is relevant
                                                                                      to many geophysical and astrophysical flows (Schumacher, Götzfried & Scheel 2015;
                                                                                      Scheel & Schumacher 2016). By exploiting the large-scale mode decomposition
                                                                                      analysis, we demonstrate that flow reversals in a tilted cell with Γ = 2 can be viewed
                                                                                      as a mode competition process between the single-roll mode and horizontally adjacent
                                                                                      double-roll mode. Reversals can only occur in an intermediate range of β where M 1,1
                                                                                      and M 2,1 are comparable. Furthermore, we find that Nuh and Nuc behave the same
                                                                                      during a reversal for Γ = 1, but they behave differently for Γ = 2 due to preference
                                                                                      for single corner roll growth during a reversal.
                                                                                         The present finding of more frequent flow reversals in a tilted Γ = 2 cell
                                                                                      demonstrates that the effects of symmetry breaking on flow reversals are system-
                                                                                      dependent, which should still be explored in asymmetrical systems to gain more
                                                                                      physical insights into the various reversal phenomena in nature.
