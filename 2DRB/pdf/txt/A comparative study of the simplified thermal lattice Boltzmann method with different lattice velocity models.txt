    ABSTRACT
    The simplified thermal lattice Boltzmann method (STLBM), as an emerging approach within the lattice Boltzmann framework, demonstrates




                                                                                                                                                                     02 June 2026 01:27:12
    exceptional potential for simulating challenging thermal flows at high Reynolds/Rayleigh numbers. While its superior numerical stability in extreme
    flow conditions has been documented, the influence of lattice velocity models on STLBM's performance remains underexplored in the literature.
    This study aims to fill this gap through comprehensive investigations into the convergence, accuracy, stability, and computational efficiency of
    STLBM with different lattice velocity models. Quantitative analyses reveal that while the model's accuracy is only marginally affected, its numerical
    stability exhibits a significant model dependency. The von Neumann stability analysis is conducted to provide theoretical explanations for
    the varied performance of numerical stability at low relaxation parameters with different lattice velocity models, in which multi-directional distur-
    bances are considered and more abundant information is provided. It is also found that, although the reduced number of lattice velocities can
    expect 14%–16% reduction of computational time in each iteration, the total simulation time could be additionally influenced by the varied conver-
    gence rates exhibited by different lattice velocity models. Finally, based on multi-criteria evaluation of stability-efficiency tradeoffs, guidelines for the
    choice of the thermal lattice velocity model in STLBM simulations of different thermal flows are provided.
