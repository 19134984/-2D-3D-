                                                                                  1. Introduction
                                                                                  As a promising new computational fluid dynamics (CFD) approach, the lattice-
                                                                                  Boltzmann equation (LBE) method has attracted much attention in engineering
                                                                                  and computational physics communities in recent years.1 Unlike its precursor —
                                                                                  the lattice-gas automata (LGA),2,3 the LBE method uses a single particle distribu-
                                                                                  tion function instead of the Boolean fluid model.4 This replacement eliminates the
                                                                                  time-consuming statistical average step, and yet retains the virtue of parallel com-
                                                                                  puting. Introduction of the single-time relaxation BGK model5 for the collisional
                                                                                  operator further simplifies the algorithm, enhances the computational efficiency, and
                                                                                  more importantly, eliminates the lack of Galilean invariance and dependence of pres-
                                                                                  sure on velocity.6,7 Theoretically, the lattice-Boltzmann equation can be proved to
                                                                                  recover the Navier–Stokes equation under the Chapman–Enskog expansion.8 Quan-
                                                                                  titative studies8,9 have shown that the LBE method is computationally more stable
                                                                                  and efficient than conventional CFD methods with a competitive accuracy.
                                                                                      Despite its exciting success in simulations of complicated flows, the previous
                                                                                  lattice-Boltzmann equation models were plagued with an undesirable feature — the
                                                                                  restriction to regular grids in physical space.10 The computational grids in previous
                                                                                  LBE models can only have a triangular or square shape in a two-dimensional domain

                                                                                  and a cubic shape in a three-dimensional domain. Undoubtedly, this restriction
                                                                                  greatly hampers the wide applications of the LBE method to practical problems.
                                                                                  This drawback is not a surprise considering that the LBE method evolves directly
                                                                                  from the LGA, in which the velocity space must be discretized into a regular lattice
                                                                                  to guarantee isotropy. Due to the Boolean nature of the lattice-gas automata, the
                                                                                  collision in LGA must take place at grid sites. Consequently, particles have to
                                                                                  hop from one grid site to another in one time step. In other words, in LGA, the
                                                                                  discretization of physical space is coupled with the discretization of velocity space.
                                                                                  Although the previous LBE models differ from the LGA model in many respects,
                                                                                  the restriction to regular lattices remained.
                                                                                      The coupling between the discretization of velocity and physical spaces is essen-
                                                                                  tial for the LGA models, but not necessary for the LBE method. This argument
                                                                                  originates from a recent discovery11,12 that, besides the LGA, the LBE method can
                                                                                  be derived from kinetic theory and the associated Boltzmann equation. Under this

                                                                                  cial discrete form of the Boltzmann equation. Based on the above facts, the author
                                                                                  and colleagues13–15 have proposed an interpolation-supplemented lattice-Boltzmann
                                                                                  equation (ISLBE) scheme which can be implemented on an irregular grid. In this
                                                                                  new scheme, the discretization of the physical space is freed from the discretization
                                                                                  of velocity space, and therefore the computational mesh can be arbitrary. The only
                                                                                  modification in the ISLBE scheme is the introduction of a new interpolation step,
                                                                                  which is necessary for reconstructing the information on each grid site after each
                                                                                  streaming process.
                                                                                      Numerical simulations using the ISLBE scheme have been carried out on flow in a
                                                                                  sudden expansion channel13,14 and flow around a cylinder.15 These studies adopted
                                                                                  typical variable grids used in other traditional CFD simulations, and still were able
                                                                                  to obtain results with good agreement with existing experimental measurements and
                                                                                  other CFD simulation results. Of course, the accuracy of the ISLBE scheme appears
                                                                                  to depend strongly on the interpolation scheme and the computational mesh. 13 To
                                                                                  ensure the numerical error is in an acceptable range, a second order interpolation
                                                                                  scheme is necessary and flow domains have to be discretized appropriately.
                                                                                      To illustrate the accuracy of this new computational scheme and its applicable
                                                                                  range, a numerical test itself is not sufficient and a theoretical analysis is always
                                                                                  desirable. It is the purpose of this paper to carry out such an analysis of the
                                                                                  numerical errors in the ISLBE scheme. This analysis will serve as a theoretical
                                                                                  justification for the ISLBE scheme. The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
                                                                                  Section 2 briefly describes the ISLBE scheme for the sake of completeness. Section 3
                                                                                  analyzes the numerical error of the ISLBE scheme for both linear and quadratic
                                                                                  interpolations. Section 4 concludes the paper.

                                                                                  4. Conclusion
                                                                                  The numerical error in the interpolation-supplemented lattice-Boltzmann BGK
                                                                                  scheme has been theoretically analyzed. The numerical error is found to depend
                                                                                  on the interpolation scheme and the way the flow domain is discretized. A linear
                                                                                  interpolation scheme is in general not appropriate for the ISLBE scheme, since it
                                                                                  inevitably introduces excessive numerical diffusion and viscosity. A quadratic inter-
                                                                                  polation scheme warrants the recovery of the Navier–Stokes equation, as long as the
                                                                                  flow domain is appropriately discretized. The analysis is consistent with previous
                                                                                  numerical experiments.
                                                                                      It is worthwhile at this point to compare the ISLBE scheme with other LBE-
                                                                                  based schemes intending to extend the LBE method to variable meshes. Two types
                                                                                  of efforts can be found along this line: the finite volume approach (FVLBE) and the
                                                                                  finite difference approach (FDLBE). The FVLBE scheme16,17 results from the mar-
                                                                                  riage of the LBE method with the finite volume technique. In the FVLBE scheme,
by NANYANG TECHNOLOGICAL UNIVERSITY on 08/24/15. For personal use only.
  Int. J. Mod. Phys. C 1997.08:737-745. Downloaded from www.worldscientific.com




                                                                                  a course-grained distribution function in each macrocell is updated according to
                                                                                  (1) the net flux of the distribution function across the cell boundaries, and (2) the
                                                                                  relaxation of the distribution function to the equilibrium state in each cell. The
                                                                                  FVLBE is an analogue of the gas-kinetic finite volume method.18 The difference is
                                                                                  that the FVLBE solves the Boltzmann equation only for a finite set of velocities,
                                                                                  while the latter solves the Boltzmann equation for all microscopic velocities. Like
                                                                                  the ISLBE, the FVLBE also requires an interpolation for the reconstruction step
                                                                                  and its accuracy strongly depends on the interpolation scheme as well. Because the
                                                                                  reconstruction is necessary along the entire cell boundaries, the FVLBE requires
                                                                                  more computation than the ISLBE for multi-dimensional problems.
                                                                                      The FDLBE scheme12 is formulated in the framework of the finite difference
                                                                                  method, in which the Boltzmann equation is solved by standard finite difference
                                                                                  schemes. This approach originates from the observation that the LBE can be
                                                                                  regarded as a first order upwind finite-difference discretization of the Boltzmann
                                                                                  equation.19,20 The LBE is of second order accuracy only because the second order
                                                                                  truncating error behaves exactly as the physical viscosity and can be absorbed into
                                                                                  the physical viscosity when a uniform grid is used. Because this absorption is no
                                                                                  longer valid for a variable grid, the FDLBE uses a second order central-difference
                                                                                  scheme to eliminate the second order truncating error. Unfortunately, the intro-
                                                                                  duction of the central-difference scheme sacrifices the upwind condition and reduces
                                                                                  the stability of the LBE scheme.
                                                                                      Finally, it should be pointed out that the analysis in this work is only appropriate
                                                                                  for the LBE scheme. The third order interpolation is not necessary for directly
                                                                                  solving the Navier–Stokes equation.
