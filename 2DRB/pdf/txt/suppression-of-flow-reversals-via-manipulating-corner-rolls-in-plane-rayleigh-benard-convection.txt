                                                                                      Suppression of flow reversals via manipulating
                                                                                      corner rolls in plane Rayleigh–Bénard convection

                                                                                      Chao-Ben Zhao1 , Bo-Fu Wang1 , Jian-Zhao Wu1, †, Kai Leong Chong1, † and
                                                                                      Quan Zhou1, †
                                                                                      1 Shanghai Key Laboratory of Mechanics in Energy Engineering, Shanghai Institute of Applied

                                                                                      (Received 27 January 2022; revised 5 July 2022; accepted 7 July 2022)


                                                                                      In this paper, we report that reversals of the large-scale circulation in two-dimensional
                                                                                      Rayleigh–Bénard (RB) convection can be suppressed by imposing sinusoidally distributed
                                                                                      heating to the bottom plate. We examine how the frequency of flow reversals depends
                                                                                      on the dimensionless wavenumber k of the spatial temperature modulation with various
                                                                                      modulation amplitude A. For sufficiently large k, the flow reversal frequency is close to
                                                                                      that in the standard RB convection under uniform heating. However, when k decreases,
                                                                                      the frequency of flow reversal gradually becomes lower and can even be largely reduced.
                                                                                      Furthermore, we examine the growth of the corner roll and the global flow structure based
                                                                                      on Fourier mode decomposition, and reveal that the size of the corner roll diminishes
                                                                                      as the wavenumber decreases. The reason is that the regions occupied by the cold phase
                                                                                      can absorb heat from the hot plumes and thus lower their temperature, which reduces the
                                                                                      corner roll’s kinetic energy input provided by the buoyancy force, and weakens the feeding
                                                                                      process of the corner rolls. This results in the locking of the corner roll into a smaller region
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.602 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      near the corner, making it harder for a reversal to occur. Using the concept of horizontal
                                                                                      convection caused by non-uniform heating, we find a relevant parameter k/A to describe
                                                                                      briefly how the reversal frequency depends on wavenumber and modulation amplitude.
                                                                                      The present work provides a new way to control the flow reversals in RB convection
                                                                                      through modifying temperature boundary conditions.
                                                                                      Key words: turbulent convection

                                                                                      Rayleigh–Bénard (RB) convection, a fluid layer heated from below and cooled from above,
                                                                                      is a simple yet widely investigated model to study the complex physics behind natural

                                                                                      convection (Ahlers, Grossmann & Lohse 2009; Lohse & Xia 2010; Chillà & Schumacher
                                                                                      2012; Sun & Zhou 2014; Shishkina 2021). Many previous studies on RB convection have
                                                                                      focused on how the heat transport efficiency and turbulent intensity depend on the control
                                                                                      parameters – which are the Rayleigh number, the Prandtl number, and the geometry of
                                                                                      the container – particularly on their scaling relationships (Ahlers et al. 2009). Apart from
                                                                                      that, the fascinating dynamics of large-scale circulation (LSC), such as its cessations and
                                                                                      reversals, have also been investigated extensively over the past few decades (Benzi 2005;
                                                                                      Brown & Ahlers 2007, 2008; Sugiyama et al. 2010; Assaf, Angheluta & Goldenfeld 2011;
                                                                                      Petschel et al. 2011; Vasiliev & Frick 2011; Wagner & Shishkina 2013; Ni, Huang & Xia
                                                                                      2015; Wang et al. 2018a; Zhang et al. 2020, 2021). In general, there are two categories of
                                                                                      flow reversals classified by their distinct physical mechanisms. In the cylindrical geometry,
                                                                                      the reversals can be caused by azimuthal rotations of the vertically circulating plane of the
                                                                                      LSC due to the azimuthal symmetry. The reversals can also be led by cessation of the
                                                                                      circulating flow (Cioni, Ciliberto & Sommeria 1997; Brown, Nikolaenko & Ahlers 2005;
                                                                                      Sun, Xi & Xia 2005; Xi, Zhou & Xia 2006). Note that the second type of reversal can take
                                                                                      place in both three-dimensional (3-D) and two-dimensional (2-D) geometries (Tsuji et al.
                                                                                      2005; Xi & Xia 2007).
                                                                                         In spite of the complicated 3-D flow dynamics such as the torsional and sloshing motions
                                                                                      of the LSC (Funfshilling, Brown & Ahlers 2008; Brown & Ahlers 2009; Xi et al. 2009;
                                                                                      Zhou et al. 2009), a (quasi-)2-D set-up was often chosen as a particular configuration to
                                                                                      study flow reversals that can minimize the 3-D flow effects. In (quasi-)2-D, the geometry
                                                                                      will limit the azimuthal position of the LSC plane, and with the removal of the complicated
                                                                                      3-D dynamics, some essential features of the reversal dynamics may be revealed (Ni
                                                                                      et al. 2015). Motivated by this, Sugiyama et al. (2010) explored, both numerically and
                                                                                      experimentally, a wide parameter space of flow reversals in 2-D and quasi-2-D square
                                                                                      boxes.They found that the growth of corner rolls plays an essential role in the reversal
                                                                                      process and then analysed the dynamics of reversals based on the energy balance of corner
                                                                                      rolls, such that we focus mainly on the reversal led by corner rolls. A Fourier-based
                                                                                      approach has also been adopted to quantify the strength of the flow rolls and their time
                                                                                      evolution (Chandra & Verma 2011). This quantitative approach also helps to unveil the role
                                                                                      of vortex reconnection in leading to the flow reversal. Besides these, a couple of theoretical
                                                                                      approaches have also been proposed to describe the physical mechanisms of the chaotic
                                                                                      flow reversals (Sreenivasan, Bershadskii & Niemela 2002; Araujo, Grossmann & Lohse
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.602 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      2005). Other recent works on flow reversals can be found in Ni et al. (2015), Podvin &
                                                                                      Sergent (2015), Wang et al. (2018b), Chen et al. (2019), Chen, Wang & Xi (2020), Zhang
                                                                                      et al. (2020, 2021) and Xu, Chen & Xi (2021).
                                                                                         In recent studies of LSC reversals, how the boundary conditions, geometries of
                                                                                      convection cells and fluid properties influence the reversal behaviours is examined. Huang
                                                                                      et al. (2015) reported that the reversal frequency could be reduced when a fixed heat flux
                                                                                      boundary condition is applied to the bottom plate of the cell. Zhang et al. (2020, 2021)
                                                                                      found that reversals of the LSC in 2-D RB convection can be suppressed or enhanced
                                                                                      by imposing local constant-temperature control on sidewalls, which offers a new way to
                                                                                      control the reversals. Xia et al. (2016) considered the non-Oberbeck–Boussinesq effects
                                                                                      and found a different mechanism for the growth-induced reversals of corner-flow rolls.
                                                                                      Wang et al. (2018a) found that the tilting of the cell tends to suppress flow reversals
                                                                                      for a convection cell of aspect ratio 1, but reversals characterized by two main rolls are
                                                                                      promoted by tilting for a larger aspect ratio. Wang et al. (2018b) and Chen et al. (2020)
                                                                                      reported a comparative experimental study of flow reversals of the LSC in an upright
                                                                                      cylindrical cell or a cornerless cell. Despite the absence of corner rolls, LSC reversals can

                                                                                      still happen, and the reversal frequency decreases with Ra. Other methods of controlling
                                                                                      the flow dynamics are also introduced, such as adding particles (Liu & Zhang 2008),
                                                                                      imposing magnetic fields (Sterl, Li & Zhong 2016), using wall roughness (Jiang et al.
                                                                                      2018; Emran & Shishkina 2020), and so on (see e.g. Sun et al. 2005; Brown & Ahlers
                                                                                      2008; Verma, Ambhire & Pandey 2015; Wang et al. 2017).
                                                                                         In this paper, we report a numerical study on how the spatially harmonic heating of
                                                                                      the bottom plate influences the flow reversals in thermal convection. In fact, turbulent
                                                                                      convection driven by a non-uniform temperature distribution is ubiquitous in nature and
                                                                                      many industrial applications, with examples including atmospheric circulation subjected
                                                                                      to spatially non-uniform heating on the Earth’s surface, and the convective flows induced
                                                                                      by spatially distributed heat sources in devices like computer chips (Freund, Pesch &
                                                                                      Zimmermann 2011; Mau, Hagberg & Meron 2012; Weiss, Seiden & Bodenschatz 2012;
                                                                                      Jovanović, Nikodijević & Nikodijević 2015; Nepomnyashchy & Simanovskii 2018). In
                                                                                      addition, it is known that non-uniform heating induces unstable vertical and horizontal
                                                                                      pressure gradients, giving rise to horizontal convection regardless of the heating intensity
                                                                                      (Hughes & Griffiths 2008; Abtahi & Floryan 2017; Yang et al. 2021b). This may affect
                                                                                      the global responses of turbulent flows and offer the possibility of controlling the pattern
                                                                                      of convection rolls. Here, we apply direct numerical simulation of 2-D convection with
                                                                                      spatially heating modulation. We explore a wide parameter space and find an extended
                                                                                      parameter regime where flow reversals can be suppressed. We show that the proposed
                                                                                      set-ups can suppress the flow reversals, depending on the wavenumber of the spatial
                                                                                      modulation, and the underlying mechanism has also been discussed.
                                                                                         The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The numerical set-ups are introduced
                                                                                      briefly in § 2. Then the results are shown in § 3. In § 3.1, we analyse the effects of spatial
                                                                                      modulation on the flow reversal frequency. In § 3.2, we investigate the behaviours of
                                                                                      flow reversals due to modulation by examining the size of corner-flow rolls. In § 3.3,
                                                                                      we adopt the Fourier mode decomposition method to distinguish clearly the difference
                                                                                      between the unmodulated and modulated reversals. In § 3.4, we rationalize the cause of
                                                                                      corner-roll suppression. In § 3.5, we introduce the concept of horizontal convection led by
                                                                                      non-uniform heating to describe how the reversal frequency depends on wavenumber and
                                                                                      amplitude. Finally, a summary is presented in § 4.

                                                                                                            3.1. Reversal frequency versus wavenumbers
                                                                                      Flow reversals can be identified through different approaches, such as the temperature
                                                                                      contrast between the left and right walls (Ni et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2019), and the sign
                                                                                      change of angular momentum (Sugiyama et al. 2010; Wang et al. 2018a; Zhang et al.
                                                                                      2020). Here, we use the latter approach, with the global angular momentum being defined
                                                                                      as (Sugiyama et al. 2010)

                                                                                                                 L(t) = (x − 0.5)w − (z − 0.5)uA ,                         (3.1)

                                                                                      where ·A represents the average over the entire 2-D plane. In figure 2, we plot the time
                                                                                      series of L(t) and the vertical slice of the horizontal velocity u at mid-width (x = 0.5)
                                                                                      for different wavenumbers k = 4, 15, 25, 30, 40, 80. In the standard RB convection
                                                                                      without spatial modulation, frequent flow reversals are observed as shown in figure 2(a),
                                                                                      namely L(t) changing its sign frequently. However, when we apply spatially harmonic
                                                                                      heating on the bottom plate, the frequency of flow reversal can reduce drastically. In cases
                                                                                      k = 4 and k = 15, one has L(t) < 0 over the whole examined time period, indicating a
                                                                                      successful elimination of the flow reversal. As the wavenumber k increases, there exists
                                                                                      an intermediate regime with a significant inhibition of flow reversals, in which the flow
                                                                                      reversal still happens, but its frequency is less than that of the standard RB convection
                                                                                      without modulation. With k increasing further, the flow reversal frequency becomes close
                                                                                      to that of the standard RB convection. This trend is also observed at small amplitudes A,
                                                                                      as shown in figure 3(a), where we plot the measured reversal frequency f as a function
                                                                                      of wavenumber k for various modulation amplitudes A. Here, the reversal frequency f
                                                                                      is defined as the averaged number of flow reversals per unit time. Moreover, it is found
                                                                                      that the intermediate regime shifts towards larger k with increasing A, suggesting that the

                                                                                      rise of amplitude A promotes the stabilization of LSC and in turn enhances the inhibition
                                                                                      ability of the reversal.

                                                                                                                 3.4. Cause of corner-roll suppression
                                                                                      So far, a question remains: how can we understand the suppression of the corner roll by
                                                                                      spatial temperature modulation? Sugiyama et al. (2010) described the role of the corner
                                                                                      flows during the reversal process. Owing to energetic feeding by the detaching plumes
                                                                                      from the boundary layers trapped in the corner, the small corner rolls will grow until they
                                                                                      reach the half-height, and then they will destroy the main LSC and establish a new LSC in
                                                                                      the opposite direction (Sugiyama et al. 2010; Zhang et al. 2020, 2021). Here, we inspect
                                                                                      closely the behaviour of thermal plumes within the corner-roll region. In figures 7(a–d),
                                                                                      we zoom into the lower-right corner-roll region, and show the instantaneous velocity
                                                                                      streamlines together with the corresponding temperature fields.
                                                                                         For the pure RB case shown in figure 7(a), the detached hot plumes are carried by the
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.602 Published online by Cambridge University Press




                                                                                      corner flow and steered to the sidewall. Then these hot plumes move upwards along the
                                                                                      sidewall, and they encounter and interact with the confronting cold plumes carried by
                                                                                      the main LSC. This flow pattern is the same as observed in previous studies (Sugiyama
                                                                                      et al. 2010; Huang & Zhou 2013; Zhang et al. 2017, 2018; Yang et al. 2021a). However,
                                                                                      for small k (e.g. k = 10), the influence of the cold zone can become significant, such that
                                                                                      the steered hot plumes lose large amounts of energy content before reaching the adiabatic
                                                                                      sidewalls. For instance, the hot plumes are not able to reach the sidewall due to the loss of
                                                                                      energy when crossing over the cold phase, as shown in figure 7(c), or even twist the moving
                                                                                      direction in the opposite one due to the effect of the cold zone, as shown in figure 7(b). This
                                                                                      effect diminishes with increasing k since the influence of the cold zone becomes limited in
                                                                                      the boundary layers and does not penetrate into the bulk. For instance, at k = 50, the cold
                                                                                      zone is too small to affect the dynamics of thermal plumes, and the corner flow resembles
                                                                                      that in the pure RB case, as shown in figure 7(d).
                                                                                         To confirm the picture of losing energy content of the hot plumes, we compare

                                                                                      (z = 0.5δCR ) shown in figure 7(e). We focus on the peak of temperature profiles at the

                                                                                      flow dynamics, i.e. it can suppress successfully and even eliminate the corner roll if k and
                                                                                      A are chosen properly. When k is very small, the corner rolls are suppressed. The majority
                                                                                      of the energy of the flow is in the single-roll mode (M 1,1 ), and the LSC is very strong.
                                                                                      Therefore, the LSC becomes robust, and it is difficult to be interrupted by corner rolls. As
                                                                                      the wavenumber k increases, the strength of corner rolls increases. The development of
                                                                                      corner rolls affects the stability of the LSC, and thus leads to the occurrence of reversals.

                                                                                                         3.5. Horizontal convection led by non-uniform heating
                                                                                      From figure 2, for sufficiently low wavenumbers, it is evident that the symmetry breaking
                                                                                      induced by spatial modulation suppresses flow reversals for A = 1. One sees that the
                                                                                      anticlockwise main roll with positive L(t) stays for quite a short time before reversing its
                                                                                      orientation. This is because for the integer values of k adopted in this study, this function
                                                                                      is asymmetric when reflected about the vertical centreline of the cavity. This introduces
                                                                                      intrinsically a horizontal non-uniformity in the buoyancy supplied through this boundary –
                                                                                      horizontal convection. Horizontal convection flows produce a bulk overturning circulation
                                                                                      at all finite Rayleigh numbers; this overturning will always rise from the hotter end of a
                                                                                      horizontal bottom boundary, and descend at the cooler end (Mullarney, Griffith & Hughes
                                                                                      2004; Sheard & King 2011; Griffiths, Hughes & Gayen 2013; Tsai et al. 2020). Therefore,
                                                                                      the spatial modulation may strengthen the clockwise LSC since along the hot and cold
                                                                                      plates, the horizontal buoyancy force is in the same direction as the flow velocity of LSC.
                                                                                      For the anticlockwise LSC, the hot plumes are more likely to move up to the bottom-left
                                                                                      corner roll, and the cold plumes tend to move down to the top-right corner roll. The two
                                                                                      corner rolls have more opportunities to grow and trigger a reversal. Thus the anticlockwise
                                                                                      LSC is less stable, it is easier to reverse its orientation, and it always stays for a much
                                                                                      shorter time, as shown in figure 2.

                                                                                      bottom boundary. Considering only the fluctuating part of the bottom temperature θ̃bot =
                                                                                      A sin(2πkx), this moment can be constructed (with k taken as integer) from

                                                                                      4. Summary
                                                                                      In summary, we have performed a comprehensive numerical study in 2-D RB convection
                                                                                      under spatially harmonic heating. We investigate the events of flow reversals from
                                                                                      the perspective of local corner-roll and global flow structures. The LSC reversals can
                                                                                      be suppressed using spatial temperature modulation when the modulation wavenumber
                                                                                      k and amplitude A are chosen properly. According to the reversal frequency f (A, k),
                                                                                      three regimes are identified in the phase diagram: the reversal-elimination regime, the
                                                                                      reversal-inhibition regime and the RB-like regime. The critical wavenumbers in between
                                                                                      the three regimes increase with increasing A. In the reversal-elimination regime where k
                                                                                      is small, the flow reversal frequency can be largely reduced with the enhanced strength of
                                                                                      LSC and the largely suppressed corner roll. In the reversal-inhibition regime with medium
