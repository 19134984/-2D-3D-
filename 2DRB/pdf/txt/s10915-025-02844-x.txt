Abstract
In this work, a Cole–Hopf transformation based fourth-order multiple-relaxation-time lattice
Boltzmann (MRT-LB) model for d-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations is developed.
We first adopt the Cole–Hopf transformation where an intermediate variable θ is intro-
duced to eliminate the nonlinear convection terms in the Burgers’ equations on the velocity
u = (u 1 , u 2 , · · · , u d ). In this case, a diffusion equation on the variable θ can be obtained,
and particularly, the velocity u in the coupled Burgers’ equations is determined by the vari-
able θ and its gradient term ∇θ . Then we develop a MRT-LB model for the d-dimensional
transformed diffusion equation, and present the corresponding macroscopic finite-difference
scheme. At the diffusive scaling, the macroscopic modified equation of the developed MRT-
LB model is derived through the Maxwell iteration method. With the aid of the free parameters
in the MRT-LB model, we find that not only the modified equation at fourth order can be
obtained, but also the gradient term ∇θ can be calculated locally by the non-equilibrium dis-
tribution function with a fourth-order accuracy, this indicates that theoretically, the MRT-LB
model for d-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations can achieve a fourth-order accuracy in
space. Finally, some simulations are conducted to test the MRT-LB model, and the numerical

1 Introduction

The Burgers’ equation, as an important kind of basic partial differential equations (PDEs),
can be used to describe some nonlinear physical phenomena, such as turbulence [35], shock
wave [8, 52], solitons [9], gas dynamics [41], traffic flow [47], nonlinear speed of sound
[20], to name but a few. In addition, the coupled Burgers’ equations, instead of a single one,
are usually adopted to describe some coupled transport phenomena. However, it should be
noted that the coupled Burgers’ equations are nonlinear, and usually, it is difficult to obtain
their analytical solutions. Thus, it is desirable and necessary to develop some numerical
methods for the (coupled) Burgers’ equation(s). In the past years, two important kinds of
numerical approaches have been developed for the (coupled) Burgers’ equation(s). The first
one is traditional macroscopic numerical methods, such as the finite-difference method [2, 39,
40], the finite-element method [50], and the finite-volume method [49]. The other one is the
mesoscopic lattice Boltzmann (LB) method, which is not only a highly efficient second-order
kinetic theory based approach for the fluid flow problems governed by the Navier–Stokes
equations [31, 55, 57, 60], but also has been successfully extended to solve the (coupled)
Burgers’ equation(s) [7, 10, 11, 23, 28, 33, 36, 38, 51, 53, 58, 64]. Actually, the existing LB
models for the (coupled) Burgers’ equation(s) [7, 10, 11, 23, 28, 33, 36, 38, 51, 53, 58, 64]
can be divided into two main kinds. The first one is the direct approach where the LB model is
developed to solve the Burgers’ equation [7, 10, 11, 23, 28, 33, 36, 38, 58, 64], however, this
approach may suffer from numerical instability due to the existence of nonlinear and coupled
convection term(s), and it is usually limited to the one- or two-dimensional Burgers’ equation
[7, 10, 11, 23, 28, 33, 36, 38, 58, 64]. The second one is the indirect approach, which is more
universal and stable, and mainly focuses on the d-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations
[51, 53]. In the second approach, the Cole–Hopf transformation [19] is used to eliminate
the nonlinearity and coupling in the d-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations, and thus a
simple diffusion equation on an intermediate variable θ is obtained. Then one can adopt some
numerical methods to solve the transformed diffusion equation [1, 44–46, 65, 66]. Moreover,
the velocity u in the coupled Burgers’ equations is further determined by the variable θ and
the gradient term ∇θ which, in the framework of LB method, can be calculated conveniently
by the first-order moment of the non-equilibrium distribution function with a second-order
accuracy [53].
    It should be noted that all the works mentioned above focus on the popular single-
relaxation-time LB (SRT-LB) model. However, the SRT-LB model becomes unstable when
the relaxation time is close to 1/2. To overcome this problem, the advanced multiple-
relaxation-time LB (MRT-LB) model [12, 13, 22] with some additional adjustable parameters
(e.g., the relaxation parameters), is more general than the SRT-LB model, and can be adopted
to achieve better numerical stability and/or accuracy through adjusting some free relaxation
parameters [21, 37, 43, 48]. For this reason, Yu et al. [63] developed a second-order MRT-LB

model for the one-dimensional Burgers’ equation. To our knowledge, however, these avail-
able LB models for the (coupled) Burgers’ equation(s) are only of second-order accuracy,
and it is still unclear whether a high-order LB model for the d(≥ 1)-dimensional (coupled)
Burgers’ equation(s) can be obtained. Actually, we note that some works have been made to
develop high-order LB models for the diffusion equations. For example, Suga [56] proposed a
fourth-order SRT-LB model with the D1Q3 lattice structure for the one-dimensional diffusion
equation, then Lin et al. [42] extended this work, and developed a sixth-order MRT-LB model.
Recently, Chen et al. [16] considered the two-dimensional diffusion equation, and proposed
a fourth-order MRT-LB model where the D2Q5 lattice structure is adopted. However, these
high-order LB models are limited to the one- and two-dimensional diffusion equations, and
there is no general high-order LB model for the d-dimensional diffusion equation. In addition,
in the framework of the Cole–Hopf transformation based LB model for the coupled Burgers’
equations, we also need to develop a high-order scheme for the gradient term ∇θ , besides the
high-order LB model for the variable θ in the diffusion equation. It should also be noted that
through the Chapman-Enskog analysis method [14], the Maxwell iteration method [34, 62],
the direct Taylor expansion method [32, 59], the recurrence equations method [27, 30] or the
equivalent equations method [24–26], one can show that the LB method is of second-order
accuracy. Besides these asymptotic analysis approaches, Bellotti et al. [5] investigated the
relation between the mesoscopic LB method and the classical macroscopic finite-difference
method, and demonstrated that the mesoscopic LB model corresponds to a macroscopic
multiple-level finite-difference scheme. They further derived the truncation errors and modi-
fied equations of the mesoscopic LB model via the macroscopic finite-difference scheme [3].
Recently, Chen et al. [18] extended the previous works [3, 5] to the general propagation LB
model. From these works [3, 18], we know that for the macroscopic finite-difference scheme
of the LB model, its modified equations at any order obtained by the Taylor expansion method
are equivalent to those obtained by the Maxwell iteration method. Based on the this fact, we
will consider the Maxwell iteration method, and propose a Cole–Hopf transformation based
fourth-order MRT-LB model for the d-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations in this work.
    The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, we first present the d-dimensional
coupled Burgers’ equations and the simple diffusion equation based on the Cole–Hopf trans-
formation. In Sect. 3, we first develop a unified framework of the MRT-LB model for the
d-dimensional transformed diffusion equation and present the corresponding macroscopic
finite-difference scheme. Then at the diffusive scaling, we derive the fourth-order modified
equation of the MRT-LB model and the fifth-order expression of the distribution function
through the Maxwell iteration method, and also the conditions of the fourth-order MRT-LB
model for the d-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations. In Sect. 4, some simulations are car-
ried out to test the accuracy of the developed MRT-LB model, and finally, some conclusions
are given in Sect. 5.

5 Conclusions

In this paper, we first adopted the Cole–Hopf transformation to eliminate the nonlinear con-
vection terms in the d(≥ 1)-dimensional coupled Burgers’ equations, and in this case, a
simple diffusion equation is obtained. In particular, the velocity u in the d-dimensional cou-
pled Burgers’ equations can be determined by the variable θ and the gradient term ∇θ in the
transformed diffusion equation. Then at the diffusive scaling, we obtained the macroscopic
finite-difference scheme of the MRT-LB model for the d-dimensional transformed diffusion
equation, and also the modified equation at fourth order of the MRT-LB model through the

be noted that the developed fourth-order MRT-LB model for the coupled Burgers’ equations

be developed for the linear diffusion equations and nonlinear coupled Burgers’ equations,
however, it is still difficult to generalize these high-order LB models to the problems with
more than a quadratic non-linearity.
